# Not All Belly Fat Is Equal: 4 Stages Every Indian Should Know

Many patients tell me, "Doctor, my sugar is normal, so my belly is not a problem." That is often not true. Belly fat moves through stages, and knowing your stage early can protect your heart and keep diabetes away.

### Stage 1 to Stage 4: how belly fat changes

Some people carry extra weight but still have normal sugar, triglycerides and HDL. Doctors call this metabolically healthy obesity. It is a stage, not a safe zone, because the World Obesity Federation now calls obesity a chronic, relapsing, progressive disease.

As the waist grows, the body slowly stops responding to insulin. First the fasting insulin rises while sugar still looks normal. Then triglycerides rise and HDL falls. Finally sugar crosses into prediabetes or diabetes.

For Indians, the warning line comes early. A waist of 90 cm or more in men, or 80 cm or more in women, is central obesity, even if your weight looks average.

### Why belly fat damages the heart

Fat stored around the belly is not just storage. It keeps releasing free fatty acids into the blood. These fatty acids block insulin's signal in muscle, so muscle takes up less sugar while the liver keeps releasing more.

Over time, this insulin resistance injures the lining of blood vessels and helps build plaques. Belly fat also releases inflammatory chemicals that make the blood more prone to clotting. This is how a growing waist becomes a heart attack or stroke risk.

### What a large study found

A study of 94,136 adults from the Kailuan community in China followed people for a median of 13 years. Being overweight raised heart disease risk by 18%, and obesity raised it by 45%. A waist of 90 cm or more in men or 85 cm or more in women raised it by 35%.

The key finding was this: nearly half (47.81%) of the extra risk in overweight people ran through insulin resistance, measured by a simple blood marker called the TyG index. The authors concluded that controlling insulin resistance can reduce the effect of obesity on the heart.

Food matters too. In an Asian population study, every extra portion of rice per day was linked to 4.62% higher HOMA-IR, and every extra portion of noodles to 6.17% higher HOMA-IR. In South India, rice is often 60% or more of our daily plate.

### What to do this week

- Measure your waist at the navel with a tape. Men aim below 90 cm, women below 80 cm.
- Get a fasting lipid profile and fasting glucose. Ask your doctor to calculate your TyG index, which is ln(triglycerides in mg/dL × fasting glucose in mg/dL ÷ 2).
- Ask for fasting insulin so your HOMA-IR can be calculated. A normal sugar with high insulin is an early warning.
- Reduce white rice by one portion a day and replace it with dal, vegetables or curd.
- Walk 30 minutes a day, ideally 10 to 15 minutes after each main meal.
